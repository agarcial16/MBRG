## Settle-edge analysis of the ranking head (owed from the M2 review; spec 025 FR-565, FR-568 of 024 FR-549)
- Question: the ranking head is the newest SETTLED counted match (its finish instant strictly below `clock - TOLERANCE_MS` at the first read). Could a match whose finish instant EXACTLY ties the head's (same ms, match id at or after the head's) join a walk mid-way?
- Conclusion: NO, the edge is closed by the settle rule as it stands; `rankingResults` and `rankingNames` need no settled bound of their own and `plan.md` needs no amendment. Argument: let `r` be the clock reading of the first read and `H` the head's instant, so `H < r - TOLERANCE_MS` (strict, `m.last_step_ms < settledBefore`). A match that finishes after the read is committed at a reading `r' >= r` (the clock does not go backward, see the lessons). Its stored instant is its SCHEDULED instant when the step ran on time, which is at most `TOLERANCE_MS` behind the effective instant, itself `>= r'` (`dueStep`, `effectiveInstant`); an overdue step takes the effective instant itself; `lastStepAfter` never lowers it. So its instant is `>= r' - TOLERANCE_MS >= r - TOLERANCE_MS > H`: STRICTLY newer than the head, so it sorts before the head in history order and the head bound ("at or after" the head) excludes it, whatever its id. A tie on `H` is therefore only possible between matches that were ALREADY stored at the first read; those are in the snapshot from its first page (the head is the smallest id among the newest instant, the keyset takes `id >=` it), and a counted match is immutable, so every page sees the same rows. The only way to break it is the one the server lesson accepts: the wall clock stepped BACKWARD by more than `TOLERANCE_MS` in the middle of a walk (an operator fault).
- Tests (`apps/server/test/ranking-snapshot.test.ts`, new, 4, explicit 120 s timeouts, mirroring `history-snapshot.test.ts`): (1) a finished match counts only once settled (the bytes of an empty ranking until `instant < now - TOLERANCE_MS`: at the instant, at the bound, then 1 ms past); (2) the tightest cases at once - a head 1 ms below the bound, a match "of a moment ago" inside the tolerance at the read (not counted, not the head), then after the first page a match with the OLDEST instant a later finish can carry (exactly `clock - TOLERANCE_MS`, 1 ms newer than the head, paying the asker 100 points and bringing a new player) and one that finishes on time 300 ms later with an instant OLDER than the match of a moment ago: the later page, the total (3) and the asker line are byte-equal to the first page's snapshot, and a fresh walk counts all of them (6 players); (3) a match at the clock of the read, or after it, is outside the walk; (4) two settled matches that tie on the head instant at the first read are both counted on every page of the walk, the same total, and a later finish at the bound does not join.
- Mutation proof: the settled bound removed from the head read (`+ 1` in place of `- TOLERANCE_MS`) -> killed by the settling test and by the snapshot test (the match of a moment ago became the head and the later older finish joined the snapshot).
