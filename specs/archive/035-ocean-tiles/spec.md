## Context and goal
User request (2026-11-24, quoted): "en general habría que partir los oceanos en bastantes "tiles" como hace EU4 por ejemplo para que las distancias no sean tan largas pero esto lo deberíamos de hacer genérico para cualquier mapa no solo para este".

Today a sea zone is one entry per curation seed (or sea unit). The sim treats a sea zone as a passable link: the absorption candidates of a faction are the land-neighbour factions **plus every living faction touching a sea zone that the faction touches**, in one group; only when that group is empty does the reach widen, **one sea zone per hop** (002 FR-75 to FR-77). A zone is therefore as long as a hop and as big as a rivalry. Measured on the hand-run outputs (2026-11-24):

| map (output) | sea zones | what one zone does |
|---|---|---|
| world-1815-6 (4,096 px, 8.59 km/px, 301 factions) | 18 | Índico: 62 land territories of 62 factions touch it (Red Sea to south Australia); Mar de la China Meridional 40 factions; Atlántico Sur 12 (West Africa to Patagonia); Pacífico Norte Oeste spans Russia's Pacific coast to northern Australia. Each is one clique of "direct rivals", and the viewer's colour assignment treats the owners around one zone as direct candidates of each other (004 FR-141), part of why the map reports `palette-exhausted` |
| spain-municipios-4 (4,096 px, 0.29 km/px, 52 factions, 8,211 land territories) | 8 | Golfo de Cádiz: 1,180 km bounding box (Huelva to Almería plus the Canary inset), 98 land territories on it; with municipalities as factions it would be one clique of 98 |

Two further measurements shape the design. (1) On the world map only about 345 million km² of the 1,093 million km² of "sea" lie within 1,500 km of land; 531 million lie beyond 3,000 km: the square frame is mostly empty water. (2) The sea passes of 028 flow by 4-connected steps, so zone borders are diamonds and diagonals, not what a human draws.

**Goal:** a generic importer step, on by default and switchable off, that splits the water of any map into many sea tiles of a controlled size, deterministic, derived from the raster only, with tile adjacency exactly pixel contact, so that factions on different continents stop being direct neighbours through one giant zone and a sea hop is a comparable distance anywhere; tiles are named after real marine areas where a names file is given. No map-format, sim, server or viewer change is required (the sim already treats `kind: "sea"` entries as ownerless links: `candidates.ts` reads sea entries only through `isSeaZone` and the neighbour graph).

What it showed: (a) **one size costs about twice the tiles for the same coast resolution** (1,150 vs 632 and 1,219 vs 619), because most of the extra tiles are empty frame; at the same count its coast is coarser (1,526 km vs 926 km, 35 vs 24 km), it has 27 to 31% fewer tiles touching land and a larger worst rival group (22 vs 16 factions on the world). (b) The reach limit alone makes **continent-long stripe tiles** along empty frame (up to 27 million km², 8,700 km): absorbing the empty ocean into the nearest tile is not enough; a third, coarse "far" tier covers it with 23 extra tiles (OQ12). (c) Real names work: of 632 world tiles 394 take a Natural Earth marine name (the rest, in the empty frame, fall back to their basin), and of 619 Spanish tiles 613 do ("mar de Alborán", "Golfo de Vizcaya", "mar Balear", "mar Tirreno", "mar de Mármara", "golfo Pérsico"); a sea that is much larger than a tile repeats its name (63 tiles of "mar de Alborán"), which is why repeated names are numbered. (d) Borders come out Voronoi-like, not diamond-shaped; the Canary inset tiles apart. (e) Prototype speed: about 5 to 10 seconds for seeding and partition at 4,096 × 4,096 in plain JavaScript (NFR-177).

## Users
- **Map author / operator**: gets tiled seas without writing anything, tunes or opts out in one line, and reviews tile counts and names in the report and the preview.
- **Spectator / predictor** (indirect): sees more plausible rivalries (neighbours across a sea are near), real sea names in panels, and a faction colouring that exhausts the palette less.

## User stories
- US-1. As a map author, I want tiling to just work on any map with no tuning, so that world, region and Spain-by-municipalities maps get sensible sea tiles.
- US-2. As a map author, I want to tune the tile count or size, keep one named sea whole, or turn tiling off, so that straits, small seas and special maps look as I want.
- US-3. As an operator, I want tiles named after the real sea or gulf they lie in, so that a match reads "mar de Alborán", not "Sea 12".
- US-4. As a spectator, I want two factions to be direct rivals across water only when their coasts are close, so that a match does not end with a faction across an ocean absorbing Asia.

## Definitions
- **Basin**: a sea zone as 028 builds it (a curation seed zone or a sea territory's units, with enclosed sets and inset water joined by FR-598, FR-601, FR-603), before tiling. **Tile**: one sea entry produced by splitting a basin.
- **Basin piece**: a 4-connected set of water pixels of one basin (an inset rectangle's water, an enclosed set joined to the zone, the main water). A basin may have several.
- **Coast distance** of a water pixel: the distance between its centre and the centre of the nearest pixel that is not water (land, other land, a lake or background), in kilometres at the map's pixel size (028 FR-609).
- **Spacing** at a pixel: `tileKm` where coast distance ≤ `coastBandKm`; `openTileKm` where it is larger and ≤ `reachKm`; `farTileKm` beyond `reachKm`.
- **Names file**: an operator-supplied GeoJSON file of named polygons (FR-694), an importer input like a context source, never part of the package.

## Functional requirements
### On by default, parameters, defaults
- FR-670: THE importer SHALL tile the sea of every import unless the curation says otherwise. `params.tiling` is optional (028 FR-600): the value `false` turns tiling off (FR-672); absent or `{}` turns it on with every default; an object may hold, all optional: `targetTiles` (integer 1 to 100,000), `tileKm`, `openTileKm`, `farTileKm`, `coastBandKm`, `reachKm` (numbers > 0, at most the frame side in km), `maxTiles` (integer 1 to 100,000, default 10,000), `zones` (an object from a basin name to a number ≥ 0: that basin's `tileKm`, and `0` meaning "keep this basin as one entry") and `names` (FR-694). A basin name is a `seas[i].name` or the name of a sea territory (028 FR-595/FR-596). Unknown members, wrong types, a spacing below 3 pixels at the map's pixel size, or a `zones` key that names no basin SHALL fail with `invalid-curation` (member path) or `unknown-sea` (name). A map with no sea zone is unaffected.
- FR-671: WHERE `tileKm` is absent, THE importer SHALL choose it so that the number of seeds of FR-674 is not above `targetTiles` and, when the search can, within 10% of it, by a bounded deterministic search, the result a whole number of kilometres rounded up (at least 3 pixels; F, the frame side in km, is 028 FR-609's). (Implementation note: the search takes at most 6 seedings; the first starts from the water area and each next scales the tile by the square root of count over target; so the first draft's exact largest count is replaced by this tolerance.) `targetTiles` defaults to `min(600, 2 × L)` with L the number of land territories (OQ11). The other spacings default from `tileKm`: `openTileKm` = 2 ×, `farTileKm` = 4 ×, `coastBandKm` = 1 ×, `reachKm` = 6 ×. Prototype result at 600 tiles: coast tile 24 km on the Spanish map, 926 km on the world map. The values in force SHALL be printed (FR-687).
- FR-672: WHERE `params.tiling` is `false`, THE importer SHALL produce exactly the package, report and errors of 028, byte for byte.

### Where it runs
- FR-673: THE importer SHALL run tiling after the water pass of 028 (zones assigned, enclosed sets joined or made lakes) and before the minimum-piece check and cleanup (028 FR-604) and the exclave bridges (028 FR-602). It SHALL change only the pixels of basins; land, other land, lakes and background are never touched. The 028 check "a sea territory has no piece of S pixels" SHALL still run on the basins as 028 FR-604 says, before tiling.

### Splitting a basin
- FR-674 (seeding): FOR each basin piece, THE importer SHALL place seeds on its water pixels so that no two seeds of one piece are closer than the spacing at the later of the two, trying candidate pixels in increasing coast distance and then row-major order. Every piece of at least 30 pixels that links to something (FR-682) therefore gets at least one seed, because the first candidate of a piece is always accepted; a piece of fewer than 30 pixels, or one that links to nothing, gets none when its basin has another piece, and FR-676 gives it to a tile (implementation note: the "centroid" rule of the first draft cannot occur and is removed). A basin with `zones[name] = 0`, or whose total pixels do not exceed one tile of its own spacing, SHALL get one tile, equal to the basin.
- FR-675 (partition): THE importer SHALL give every water pixel of a basin piece to the tile of the seed nearest to it by distance through the piece's own water (a tile never reaches across land or into another basin), ties to the seed first in seed order. The distance SHALL be Euclidean-like, not a count of 4-connected steps: on open water between two seeds the border SHALL be the perpendicular bisector within 2 pixels for seeds up to 100 pixels apart (a 5-7-11 chamfer, within 1.5% of Euclidean: implementation note), so borders show no horizontal, vertical or 45° bias; a step across a pinch point of the piece is refused.
- FR-676: IF a tile has fewer pixels than `max(S, 0.1 × spacing²)` (S = `minPieceSize`, spacing in pixels at its seed), THEN THE importer SHALL merge it into the tile of the same piece that shares the longest border with it (ties to the lower tile order); a piece with fewer than 30 pixels and no seed of its own SHALL join the tile of its basin whose seed is nearest by squared distance between pixel centres (a tile being an id and not a connected region, as a zone is). Therefore no tile fails `territory-below-min-size` (028 FR-604) because of tiling.
- FR-677: THE importer SHALL number tiles of one basin 1..n in order of the row-major position of their seed pixel (north to south, then west to east), after merges.

### Ids, names, adjacency, sea semantics
- FR-678: THE importer SHALL make each tile a sea entry (`kind: "sea"`, no owner) numbered by 028 FR-605: sea ids come after land ids, basins in the order of FR-605, the tiles of one basin consecutive in tile order. A basin that stays whole keeps today's single id and name.
- FR-679: THE importer SHALL name each tile as follows. WHERE a names file is given (FR-694), a tile takes the name of the polygon that covers most of its pixels, counting for each pixel the smallest-area polygon that covers its centre (so a bay inside a sea never swallows the sea), ties to the polygon with the smaller area, then the lower feature index; a tile none of whose pixels is covered takes its basin's name; WITHOUT a names file every tile takes its basin's name. Names used by more than one tile (after that step) SHALL be numbered `<name> <n>` (one space, `n` in decimal without padding, from 1, in the order of the tile numbers of FR-677 across the map); a name used by one tile stays as it is. A basin that stays whole keeps its name. Names are used exactly as given (never trimmed or normalised, 028 FR-593). IF a name would exceed 80 code points (003a FR-87), THEN `tile-name-too-long` (name, length). Territory names may repeat anyway (003a).
- FR-680: THE importer SHALL derive every link, tile-tile, tile-land and across basins, only from the final pixel contact (003a FR-108, 028 FR-605), with no field and no inserted link. Two tiles are neighbours if and only if some pair of their pixels is 4-adjacent; tiles of different basins that touch (a strait) are linked.
- FR-681: THE importer SHALL NOT change the map format, `MatchState`, the protocol or the sim, and tiles SHALL never be owned: they are passable links, as sea zones are (002 FR-57, FR-59, FR-72). Their effect on a match is only through the graph: factions are direct candidates when they touch a common tile, and a widening hop crosses one tile.

### Interaction with the other 028 steps
- FR-682: Lakes (enclosed sets with no zone within `enclosedReach`, 028 FR-603) SHALL stay other land and never be tiled. An enclosed set that joined a basin is a piece of that basin and is split by FR-674, except a piece that touches no land territory and no other basin (water enclosed in other land): it would be a tile that links to nothing and the map would fail `disconnected-map`, so it joins the nearest tile of its basin by squared distance between pixel centres, as it joined the zone in 028 (implementation note, found by the existing import tests).
- FR-683: Water of an inset rectangle that is a sea apart or that names its sea (028 FR-601) belongs to its basin and is split as pieces of that basin; no tile SHALL cross the rectangle's border and no seed outside the rectangle SHALL serve a tile inside it.
- FR-684: Exclave bridges (028 FR-602) SHALL run on the tiles as on sea zones: the line may cross tiles, the water pixels 4-adjacent to it become other land, a tile cut into pieces is cleaned by the piece cleanup of 028 FR-604, and `unreachable-exclave` is unchanged. A bridge SHALL never be counted as water in the report.
- FR-685: IF the total number of tiles would exceed `maxTiles`, THEN `too-many-tiles` (cap, count, the basins with the most tiles) SHALL fail the import at the pixel stage before any partition is computed; the viewer limits of 028 FR-607 stay warnings (entries above 65,535, required texture size) and now count tiles in "entries".

- FR-686: THE report (028 FR-609) SHALL gain a tiling section: the spacings, band and reach in kilometres and in pixels; per basin, its pixels, its area in km², its pieces and its tiles; the totals; the smallest, median and largest tile (km²); the largest number of factions touching one tile; every merged tile; the number of tiles named from the names file and the number that fell back to a basin name; and the SHA-256 of the names file. The viewer-limits section SHALL be computed on the final entries.
- FR-687: THE report SHALL print the tiling parameters in force (FR-671), also when all are defaults.
- FR-688: THE repository SHALL hold a small committed synthetic sample for tiling (an ocean with two coasts, a strait between two basins, one enclosed set joined to a basin, an inset with a sea, one basin kept whole by `zones`, a bridged exclave, a names file with a bay inside a sea) whose package is compared byte for byte by a test; it SHALL NOT be added to `maps/`, the server settings or the e2e harness.
- FR-689: A tiling whose `tileKm`, `openTileKm`, `farTileKm`, `coastBandKm` and `reachKm` are at least the frame side SHALL produce exactly the package of the same curation with `tiling: false` (every basin stays whole), proving the step is neutral at its limit.
- FR-690: THE deployment guide SHALL gain the operator steps for tiling: that it is on by default and `tiling: false` turns it off, `targetTiles` and the spacings, `zones`, the names file (where to get Natural Earth's marine areas, its licence note, FR-694), how to read the section of FR-686 and the viewer-limits warnings, and that unnamed tiles carry the basin's name plus a number.

- FR-692: THE spec's feature PR SHALL record on `docs/board.md` the follow-up V4 (viewer: how a map with hundreds of sea tiles is drawn: the sea-zone border weight, optional sea-name labels) only if the hand run's screenshots show the need (OQ9).
- FR-693 (goldens, OQ1): THE curation of the dev-map equivalence proof (028 FR-611) SHALL set `tiling: false`, because that proof is about reproducing the 003b pipeline, which has no tiling, and `maps/dev` and every predefined map under `maps/` (all built by the builder, not the importer) SHALL stay byte for byte; the 028 sample (FR-612) SHALL run with default tiling and its expected package SHALL be regenerated in the feature PR with the diff reviewed (it now exercises the default path); the new sample of FR-688 covers the special cases.
- FR-694 (names file): THE `params.tiling.names` member SHALL be `{ file, nameProperty }`: `file` a path relative to the curation file to a GeoJSON `FeatureCollection` of `Polygon` and `MultiPolygon` features read under 028 FR-587 and FR-588 (same caps, `source-too-large` and the geometry errors), `nameProperty` a list of 1 to 3 property names, the first of them present as a valid name (003a FR-87) being the feature's name, a feature with none being skipped (Natural Earth: `["name_es", "name"]`). Its polygons SHALL be projected and rasterised by the same centre-point rule and views as context features (028 FR-600, FR-601), with the same ignore rule beyond 90° for `laea`; a polygon the projection cannot draw SHALL be reported as a warning and skipped, never fail the import. The file is an input like a context source: no new dependency (NFR-176), and its licence travels with every map built from it (028 FR-613).

- FR-696: IF the names file gives two tiles of one basin the same name, THEN they are numbered (FR-679); IF a name already numbered by the author in the file (for example "Estrecho de Gibraltar 2") collides with a generated one, THEN the generated number skips it, so that every tile name in the package is unique.

## Non-functional requirements
- NFR-175 (Determinism and purity): the tiling core takes the raster, parameters and the parsed names file and gives the raster and names; no file system, clock, randomness or network (028 NFR-134); two runs give identical bytes; the purity scan covers the new sources.
- NFR-176 (Compatibility and data): `maps/dev`, all goldens, the format, the server and the viewer are unchanged (028 NFR-132); no new dependency (028 NFR-133); the names file is data supplied by the operator (Natural Earth marine polygons are public domain: 306 features, 295 with a Spanish name, 1.7 MB as GeoJSON; whether to commit a copy is OQ13).
- NFR-177 (Performance): on the scale fixture of 028 NFR-136 (4,096 px, 8,191 entries) with default tiling, tiling adds at most 10 seconds to the import (the total stays within the 120 s bound), single-threaded, default heap, on the development machine run alone. Implementation note: the first draft said "at most 25% to the import time"; measured, the fixture imported in 46.4 s before this spec and the piece labelling shared with 003b was a closure-per-pixel loop that cost most of it; making it a plain loop with identical results took the import without tiling to 15.7 s and with tiling to 24.3 s (tiling adds 8.6 s: seeding and search about 2 s, partition about 3 s, the rest of its passes and the larger cleanup the others), so the budget is now stated in seconds.
- NFR-178 (Language): code, error kinds and report labels in English; the numeric tile suffix is language-neutral; names are the dataset's or the author's content.
- NFR-179 (Testability): tests first for every id; fixtures at real scale (a 1,000-pixel-wide ocean, a 4,096 world slice with empty frame); each rule has a test that fails when the rule is removed (mutation check on the seeding order, the tie rule, the merge threshold, the far tier and the smallest-polygon rule of FR-679).

## Edge cases
- Empty frame (corners, poles): covered by a few large far-tier tiles (FR-674), never by continent-long stripes.
- A basin smaller than one tile, a one-pixel enclosed set, a basin with `zones: 0`: one entry, today's name.
- Gibraltar-like strait between two basins: tiles of both touch; the link exists whatever the tile size.
- Canary Islands inset (`inset.sea` naming the Gulf of Cádiz basin): the rectangle is its own piece, tiled apart; its tiles get names from the names file where the inset's own projection places them (the prototype reconstructed them approximately) or the basin's name.
- A sea much larger than a tile (the 195 × 395 km "mar de Alborán" holds 63 tiles of 24 km): the same real name repeats and is numbered; the author raises `tileKm` for the map or lowers density for it with `zones`.
- Names file polygons covering a tile only partly or not at all (open ocean beyond the dataset, the empty frame of a world map): fallback to the basin's name.
- A map with no sea zones: tiling does nothing. A small map (dev map, 70 land territories): `targetTiles` is 140, not 600.
- Spain by municipalities: about 600 tiles on 8,211 land territories raise the entries to about 8,800 and the required texture size from 8,220 to about 8,800 (both above 8,192): reported, never a gate (028 OQ5).
- Exclave group on an island: its bridge crosses tiles (028 FR-602); tiles cut by it are cleaned.

## Out of scope
- Any change to the sim, shared package, protocol, server, bot or viewer, including sea-name labels, a different sea-zone border style and per-tile art (OQ9 records them as follow-up V4).
- Sea ownership or sea combat; tiles are passable links only.
- A tile-to-basin grouping field in the map or presentation format (OQ7, answered NO).
- Hand-placed tile seeds or per-tile names in the curation; tiling lakes; subdividing land territories; hierarchical (multi-level) sea graphs.
- Translating or normalising names from the dataset (some Natural Earth Spanish names are English, for example "Viscount Melville Sound"); the author edits the file.
